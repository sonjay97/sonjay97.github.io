My First PyCon (2026)

Published: May 13, 2026

Day 1, Wednesday May 13

I recently got a new job that allows me the flexibility to work while attending conferences (incredible). One conference I have been meaning to go to since I first started my career as a software engineer is PyCon. I recently attended PyCon 2026 and I want to share my experiences below.

I decided to go into this conference with the idea of getting the "full experience", meaning I stay for the full duration and participate in everything offered, including the sprint sessions at the end of the conference. So I signed up for tutorials, went to talks, and attended the PyLadies dinner auction thing. I also read a guide from RealPython on what to expect at PyCon in general. This made me feel good about the conference but realistically I knew that I had to actually attend to see what was up.

I attended a tutorial on the first day. It was on creating MCP servers and was awesome. This was on the May 13th morning and I learned a great deal about what it looks like to actually integrate MCP servers. My job has asked me to make a lot of AI tools recenlty, and I've unknowingly developed many MCP-like tools without formally knowing what MCP are. Attending a class, seeing what the industry has available for developing MCP servers, and then doing the development hands on with developers beside me was helpful in getting me up and running with MCP in general. I now feel like I can take this back to my team and incorporate this with our goals. I would say this tutorial was definitely worht it.

The second tutorial I attended in the afternoon was OK at best. I attended a tutorial on RAG with Python, and learned a lot of stuff I already knew. While it was nice to pick up a few new tidbits on RAG like what libraries others are using and what that code look like, I think the method of doing live coding demos gets pretty messy no matter how much you prepare, which is what ended up happening for the presenter. This is probably one of the more important take aways I had at PyCon: Not every talk is created equally. A topic that seems un interesting can actually be incredible based on the presenters ability to publically speak and present. I think having the code already written and tested is a much better way to prevent live demo issues (we all know the story: "it was working on my machine a few minutes ago I swear!")

Day 2, Thursday May 14

Day 2 was a day for tutorials in the morning and sponsor presentations in the afternoon. The opening reception was at night. I attended a tutorial on some machine learning models and some sponsor presentations on building AI agents. Both of these talks were moderately useful. I didn't learn much from either, but what really felt impactful was meeting a few friendly developers in the hallway that shared their professional opinions on AI with me. I learned a lot about how other companies are using AI and what limitations these teams are seeing. This would be a theme in the coming days that I really appreciated out of PyCon, getting to meet friendly developers who have a real passion for the game.

Day 3, Friday May 15

Day 3 was awesome. The expo floor opened up and I got an insane amount of free company swag including t-shirts, socks, hats, and backpacks (thanks Tower financial!). Copius amounts of stickers too :). I really enjoyed getting to chat with different companies about their products and how they do stuff. While the conversations were really just sales pitches, it was cool to see so many big companies in one place. Also met some more developer friends who I tagged along with to get even more free swag. Felt a lot like Trick o' treat on Halloween. I also attended some proper talks on various topics that ranged from mildly amusing to truly informative. Learned on Friday that there are a lot of outcomes that come from these talks! and to take it all as it comes. No judgement here. Speaking in front of an audience on work that you've done is always a bit unnerving so I empathize with the speakers and commend them for what they are doing.. trying!

Day 4, Saturday May 16

Attended more talks, a keynote speech, and the PyLadies Auction. Almost bid $850 on a GitHub Skate deck which I kind of regret not spending more for. Oh well. maybe next year. I did spend a lot on attending this conference in the first place so I was hesitant to spend another $1k haha. I think the coolest thing I learned on Saturday was just how much I had been missing out on from the Python community. Seeing so many people share their unique projects and experiences really highlighted something crucial for me: There is a lot of engineering I need to start doing in my free time. Seeing others develop such cool new software with Python made me realize that I need to get busy implementing my own half baked ideas I've been musing over the past three years. Here in front of me at this conference were hundreds of people who had ideas and made them a reality. I was truly inspired. I guess when I had initially done my career change from pre med to software engineering I had kept a lot of the "pre med" mentality which is "study study study" to pass tests, quizzes, and labs. But really, software engineering could not be more different as a discipline. This field is all about those who take chances to create something wonderful. Your real world development matters more than anything! I was witnessing a change within myself, seeing others do such cool projects stoked a fire in me that was always a tiny little pilot light. I felt good and realized just how buys I want to be writing code. Thanks PyCon.

This blog is a good example of what I mean, I had always wanted to have a tech blog like so many others I see on hacker news. But I never really wrote up any of the articles I had been meaning too. PyCon really opened my eyes to what I've been missing. A community! Seeing people who were actually interested in what I had to say made me more comfortable with getting back into actually working on my tech blog. Neat.

Day 5 & 6, Sunday May 17 - Monday May 18

I'm grouping the last two days together because they kind of blurred together in my mind. Sunday was the unofficial official last day of the conference since so many had to go home and get ready for a work day on Monday. I had decided to stay one more day for the sprints. Sunday was a really tiring day for me, I was pooped from all the previous days and waking up early, but I still pushed on. Watched the last keynote and went to some more talks, they kinda fell flat for me though. Still I said goodbye to the many new friends I made and called it a success. Monday was the sprint day and I learned alot about Document DB, a open source offering from the folks at Microsoft. Open source contributions was another thing I had always dreamed of doing but never really new how to start. Being able to talk to the fine folks at Microsoft gave me the much needed confidence to start actually reading about their issues and code base in order to make my first PR. While I am still learning about DocumentDB I am confident now that I can reach out to the maintainers I met with any questions I have and they would be more than happy to help me out. Neat. Thanks PyCon!

Conclusion

PyCon taught me something that I never knew I was missing from my life as a profession software engineer. Community drives us forward much farther and much faster than anything we do alone. Just attending talks and talking to other developers made me realize just how sorely I needed good ol regular conversation about what I do, how I do it, and what to do next. I've sadly worked in a lot of toxic work environments in the past with god awful developers and can safely say I met some of the nicest and smartest devs at PyCon which renewed my faith as a developer. I really look forward to the next PyCon and am happy to say I am now firing on all cylinders working on all my projects again. Looking forward to sharing more about them on my blog in the future.

-Jay
