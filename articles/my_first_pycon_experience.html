My First PyCon (2026)

Published: May 13, 2026

Day 1, Wednesday May 13

I recently got a new job that allows me the flexibility to work while attending conferences (incredible). One conference I have been meaning to go to since I first started my career as a software engineer is PyCon. I attended PyCon 2026 and I wanted to share my experiences below.

I decided to go into this conference with the idea of getting the "full experience" meaning I stay for the full duration and participate in everything offered. So I signed up for tutorials on the 13th and decided to stay an extra day to participate in the sprints on the 19th. I also read a guide from RealPython on what to expect at PyCon in general. This made me feel good about the conference but realistically I knew that I had to actually attend to see if it was worth it or not.

My first tutorial on creating MCP servers was awesome. This was on May 13th morning and I learned a great deal about why MCP servers are useful. My job has asked me to make a lot of AI tools and I've unknowingly developed many MCP-like tools without formally knowing what MCP is. Attending a class, seeing what the industry has available for developing MCP servers, and then doing the development hands on with developers beside me was helpful in getting me up and running with MCP in general. I now feel like I can take this back to my team and incorporate this with our goals. I would say this tutorial was definitely worht it.

The second tutorial I attended in the afternoon was OK at best. I attended a tutorial on RAG with Python, and learned a lot of stuff I already knew. While it was nice to pick up a few new tidbits on RAG like what libraries others are using and what that code look like, I think the method of doing live coding demos gets pretty messy no matter how much you prepare, which is what ended up happening for the presenter. This is by no means me trying to insult the presenter, just more so the critiquing the method of delivery for teaching the audience on what RAG is. I think having the code already written and tested is a much better way to prevent live demo issues (we all know the story: "it was working on my machine a few minutes ago I swear!")
